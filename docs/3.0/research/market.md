# GlobaLens 3.0: Competitive Landscape, Inspiration & White Space (as of 2026-10-28)

> **Method.** About 45 targeted web searches plus about 25 direct fetches of primary sources (official sites, pricing pages, GitHub, research blogs, release notes). The shared web-search budget ran out partway through. After that, claims were checked by fetching primary pages directly or are marked **[memory]** (from prior knowledge, not re-verified) or **[unverified]**. Confidence levels: **H** high / **M** medium / **L** low.

## TL;DR
- **"3D globe + live news + AI summaries" already exists as a viral open-source product.** World Monitor (`koala73/worldmonitor`): 89.7k stars, AGPL-3.0, desktop app, MCP server, SDKs, Pro at $39.99/mo. "Another situation-room dashboard" will read as a clone (H).
- **"AI summary of today's news" is cheap, and platforms are licensing it.** Google pays 400+ publications in its News AI pilot; Perplexity gives publishers 80% of Comet Plus revenue **[corrected: Perplexity's own post (25 Aug 2025) says publishers get all Comet Plus revenue minus a small share for compute; the 80% split and $42.5M pool are press-reported figures not found in a primary source]**; OpenAI retired ChatGPT Pulse (H/M).
- **Trust is the unsolved problem.** In the EBU/BBC study, 45% of AI-assistant news answers had a significant issue; Apple paused its news summaries (H). Cited, publicly evaluated AI is a real differentiator.
- **Every serious data product now offers MCP** (World Monitor, Perigon, GDELT Cloud, Felt, Esri, kepler.gl). **[corrected: the claim that none of them offers geospatial + temporal + cross-lingual-perspective tools for news is wrong. World Monitor's MCP added bbox/radius + `since_days` filters for conflict and disaster events (20 Oct 2026) and `get_media_perspective` (27 Oct 2026), which groups headlines about a country by outlet home country, UN M49 region and audience. Perigon's API supports `lat`/`lon`/`maxDistance` radius search and its MCP has `get_location_news` and `search_story_history`. Still missing from every MCP checked: per-story framing diffs with cited claims, propagation metrics, attention-vs-severity, and H3/time-bucket aggregates (M)]**
- **The opening GlobaLens can own is "how the world sees the world":** cross-lingual framing comparison, how a story spreads across countries over time, and coverage versus severity ("forgotten crises"). All three would be driven by the Lens, replayable in time, and queryable by agents. **[corrected: World Monitor started moving into this space on 27 Oct 2026 (country-level media perspective via API/MCP; its translated titles and LLM stance labels are defined in the schema but currently returned empty), so the opening is depth — per-story framing diffs, propagation, attention gaps — not the category itself]**

### 2e. Open building blocks
- **Media Cloud 2.0** (ICWSM, 25 May 2026): open-source, open-data archive with more than 1.8B stories, a source Directory and a Story Index; collections cover 100+ countries. Useful for outlet metadata.
- **Event Registry / NewsAPI.ai**: 150,000+ sources in 60+ languages, grouped into events. B2B; pricing by contact.
- **Perigon**: Stories API (clusters with summaries, sentiment, entities and spread counts) plus an official MCP server.
- **UCDP**: GED v26.1 plus monthly Candidate events (lag of at most a month), **CC BY 4.0**.
- **VIEWS**: open-access monthly conflict forecasts for the next 1–36 months, by country-month and on a 0.5° grid for Africa and the Middle East.
- **ACLED CAST**: 6-month forecasts behind a free myACLED login. Redistribution terms still need checking.
- **Bellingcat Online Investigations Toolkit**: community-maintained, 12 categories (Maps & Satellites, Geolocation, Archiving…). A source of verification ethos and conventions.
- **Jigsaw Unfiltered.News** (2016): mapped which topics were under-reported in each country using Google News data; **now defunct**. Proof the idea resonates, and that nobody kept it alive.

## 3. Forces that changed the field since v1 (2025–26)
1. **The dashboard is a commodity.** A solo developer plus AI coding can now ship a 40-layer situation room (World Monitor).
2. **Platforms absorb and license summaries** (Google's paid pilot, Perplexity's 80/20 share, Pulse retired).
3. **There is a trust deficit.** EBU/BBC: 45% of answers had a significant issue, 31% had sourcing problems, 20% had major accuracy problems.
4. **Distribution is becoming agent-native.** MCP is everywhere. **WebMCP** (a draft first published 13 Aug 2025 by Microsoft and Google authors; still experimental) lets a web page register tools for agents via `document.modelContext.registerTool()`. **[corrected: the explainer was merged 13 Aug 2025, but the formal Community Group spec draft dates from Jan 2026 (latest draft 27 Oct 2026). Chrome exposes it through an origin trial from Chrome 149 (per World Monitor's docs). World Monitor (Aug 2026) and kepler.gl's 3.3.0 alphas already ship WebMCP integrations, so it is no longer a differentiator]**
5. **Content access is tightening.** Since 1 Jul 2025 Cloudflare, which fronts about 20% of the web, blocks AI crawlers by default.
6. **Geospatial agents are maturing.** Google reports that planning plus domain tools beat standalone LLMs on spatial tasks.

## 6. Table stakes (expected; they don't differentiate)
- Smooth globe on mid-range laptops and a usable mobile layout; clustering and heatmaps; level-of-detail. Stream data incrementally (not v1's 2,000-document JSON dump).
- Keyword and semantic search; filters for time, place, theme, language, source country and tone.
- Story clustering and deduplication, with a source list, outbound links and freshness stamps ("updated 6 min ago").
- AI summaries with inline citations, an "AI-generated" label, a story timeline and "why it matters".
- Outlet transparency (country, language, owner from Wikidata) instead of borrowed proprietary bias scores.
- Watchlists and alerts, saved views, shareable deep links with the full state in the URL, preview images when shared.
- Accessibility: keyboard navigation, a reduced-motion mode, a list/table alternative to the globe, color-blind-safe palettes, light and dark themes, UI localization.
- Public API docs, MCP, rate limits, a status page, privacy-respecting analytics.

## 7. What GlobaLens should *be* (product direction)
- **Positioning:** "**GlobaLens: see how the world sees the world**" (keep "See beyond the headlines" as the secondary line). A *perspective, propagation and attention engine* for world news. The Lens is the universal query, time is a first-class dimension, and agents are first-class users.
- **Interview narrative:** "Google News Full Coverage × Google Earth × Jigsaw's Unfiltered.News, rebuilt open and agent-native."
- **Personas:** (1) globally curious readers and students; (2) journalists, researchers, OSINT hobbyists; (3) AI agents via MCP. **Not** enterprise security teams: their vendors' moats are human verification and sales teams.
- **Non-goals:** flight, ship and market layers (World Monitor owns breadth, and ADS-B/AIS feeds bring cost and licensing problems); proprietary bias ratings; scraping or storing full article text; opaque "instability scores" with no backtests; claims of autonomous forecasting.
- **What a solo developer can defend:** (a) the cross-lingual clustering pipeline over the 15-minute firehose; (b) evaluation rigor made public; (c) a signature interaction (Lens + time + agent) that looks great in a screen recording.

## Recommendations for GlobaLens 3.0
1. **MUST: Reposition around perspective, propagation and attention, with the Lens as the universal query.** World Monitor owns the generic dashboard; platforms own summaries.
2. **MUST: Make the cross-lingual story-cluster pipeline the core system.** Ingest GDELT's 15-min raw files, then dedupe, embed (multilingual), and cluster. Each cluster carries countries, languages, first-seen times, tone, entities and geocode precision. Features 2–5, 8, 9 and 12 all depend on it, and it is the data/ML depth story.
3. **MUST: Build trust into the design.** Sentence-level citations, AI labels, the Location Honesty Layer, and a public Trust Report from early phases on.
4. **MUST: Use metadata first, link out, and never store or redistribute full text.** Use only RSS and official APIs and respect AI-crawl preferences. This fixes v1's copyright exposure and matches the 2025–26 licensing climate.
5. **MUST: Declare the non-goals above in the README.** Focus is what reads as seniority.
6. **SHOULD: Be agent-native.** Ship an MCP server with geo/time/framing tools and put the full app state in deep-linkable URLs. Add WebMCP experimentally once the core is stable. **[corrected: World Monitor already ships 33 WebMCP tools, so treat WebMCP as table stakes, not a differentiator]**
7. **SHOULD: Ship Time Machine + Propagation Replay as the visual "wow"** (pre-aggregated time buckets; arcs by first-seen time).
8. **SHOULD: Ship the Silence Map on open data** (UCDP CC BY + Kontur population). It is socially resonant and easy to demo.
9. **SHOULD: Make Early-Warning Radar the ML showpiece, with published backtests.** Show VIEWS forecasts as context, never as GlobaLens's own forecasts.
10. **SHOULD: Build Ask the Globe** **[corrected: World Monitor's Analyst already drives its map; differentiate on narrated tours, lens/time actions and sentence-level citations]** using typed UI actions, per-session token budgets and an eval set (borrowing the idea of Google's GeoKTools-style benchmarks).
11. **COULD: Add growth loops:** Lens Briefings, News Diet Passport, Globe Cast, embeddable cards.
12. **COULD: Choose a permissive license (Apache-2.0 or MIT).** This sets GlobaLens apart from World Monitor's AGPL plus paid MCP; protect hosting costs with rate limits rather than with the license.
